References and Further Reading

Cited in this course

The BMJ “Diagnostic tests” series by Altman and Bland remains the clearest short introduction to the subject, and is worth reading in full before anything longer (Altman and Bland 1994a, 1994b; Deeks and Altman 2004).

For anyone appraising or designing a diagnostic accuracy study, STARD sets out what must be reported (Bossuyt et al. 2015) and QUADAS-2 gives the framework for judging risk of bias (Whiting et al. 2011). The clinical definitions used throughout come from the fourth universal definition of myocardial infarction (Thygesen et al. 2019). The interpretation of the area under the ROC curve follows Hanley and McNeil (Hanley and McNeil 1982), and the confidence intervals use the methods compared by Newcombe (Newcombe 1998).

Altman, Douglas G., and J. Martin Bland. 1994a. “Diagnostic Tests 1: Sensitivity and Specificity.” BMJ 308 (6943): 1552. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.308.6943.1552.
———. 1994b. “Diagnostic Tests 2: Predictive Values.” BMJ 309 (6947): 102. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.309.6947.102.
Bossuyt, Patrick M., Johannes B. Reitsma, David E. Bruns, Constantine A. Gatsonis, Paul P. Glasziou, Les Irwig, Jeroen G. Lijmer, et al. 2015. “STARD 2015: An Updated List of Essential Items for Reporting Diagnostic Accuracy Studies.” BMJ 351: h5527. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.h5527.
Deeks, Jonathan J., and Douglas G. Altman. 2004. “Diagnostic Tests 4: Likelihood Ratios.” BMJ 329 (7458): 168–69. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.329.7458.168.
Hanley, James A., and Barbara J. McNeil. 1982. “The Meaning and Use of the Area Under a Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) Curve.” Radiology 143 (1): 29–36. https://doi.org/10.1148/radiology.143.1.7063747.
Newcombe, Robert G. 1998. “Two-Sided Confidence Intervals for the Single Proportion: Comparison of Seven Methods.” Statistics in Medicine 17 (8): 857–72. https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1097-0258(19980430)17:8<857::AID-SIM777>3.0.CO;2-E.
Thygesen, Kristian, Joseph S. Alpert, Allan S. Jaffe, Bernard R. Chaitman, Jeroen J. Bax, David A. Morrow, and Harvey D. White. 2019. “Fourth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction (2018).” European Heart Journal 40 (3): 237–69. https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehy462.
Whiting, Penny F., Anne W. S. Rutjes, Marie E. Westwood, Susan Mallett, Jonathan J. Deeks, Johannes B. Reitsma, Mariska M. G. Leeflang, Jonathan A. C. Sterne, and Patrick M. M. Bossuyt. 2011. “QUADAS-2: A Revised Tool for the Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies.” Annals of Internal Medicine 155 (8): 529–36. https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-155-8-201110180-00009.

Suggested next steps

For teaching colleagues who want to extend this material:

  • Diagnostic meta-analysis — pooling sensitivity and specificity across studies requires bivariate models, because the two measures are negatively correlated through the threshold.
  • Multiple tests in sequence — how likelihood ratios combine when tests are conditionally independent, and what goes wrong when they are not.
  • Net benefit and decision curve analysis — a formal extension of the cost-weighting shown in Chapter 4.
  • Prediction models — calibration, discrimination and the difference between a good model and a useful one.
